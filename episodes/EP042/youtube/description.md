The Dot-Com Bubble by the Numbers (and the 2026 Echo)

In 1999, a company whose main asset was a sock puppet was worth over $300 million — and gone a year later. This is the dot-com bubble, by the numbers: the internet mania, 'eyeballs' replacing profits, the IPO frenzy, Greenspan's ignored 'irrational exuberance' warning, the Nasdaq's March 2000 peak and ~78% crash — and the survivors, like Amazon, that rose from the rubble. Then the uncomfortable question: is the 2026 AI boom rhyming with all of it? Fully cited.
