Mutual Funds vs Index Funds vs ETFs vs Hedge Funds

One fund charges you almost nothing. Another charges 2% a year plus 20% of your profits — and still loses to the cheap one. Yet they often own nearly the same things. This is mutual funds vs index funds vs ETFs vs hedge funds, by the numbers: what each one actually is, how you buy them (once-a-day vs live), the fees (expense ratios and '2 and 20'), who's even allowed in, and the blunt data on which one wins for ordinary people. Fully cited.

IN THIS VIDEO
• The one idea all four share: pooling money into a basket
• Mutual funds — the classic, priced once a day
• Index funds — a cheap style, not a separate rival
• ETFs — index funds that trade live like a stock
• Hedge funds — '2 and 20', accredited-only, and often behind the index
• Who actually wins: why cost is the best predictor of returns
