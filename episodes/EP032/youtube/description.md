Madoff by the Numbers: How $65 Billion Never Existed

Bernie Madoff was the most trusted man on Wall Street — a former Nasdaq chairman whose fund rose smoothly, about 11% a year, for three decades. It was the largest Ponzi scheme in history. The $65 billion on investors' statements was almost entirely invented: no trades, no profits, just new investors' money paying old ones. This is Madoff by the numbers — how the con worked, the whistleblower the SEC ignored for nine years, the 2008 collapse, the 150-year sentence, and the $14 billion clawback. Fully cited.

IN THIS VIDEO
• What a Ponzi scheme actually is — and why it always needs new money
• How Madoff did it: credibility, exclusivity, feeder funds, and fake statements
• The tells: returns too smooth to be real, and math that didn't exist
• Harry Markopolos — the analyst who warned the SEC for ~9 years
• The 2008 collapse: $7B in redemptions he couldn't pay
• $65B on paper vs ~$17B really lost — and the ~$14B clawed back
